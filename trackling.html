Trackling

Helping older adults build safer walking routines during isolation.

Trackling is a mobile fitness concept designed for older adults who wanted to stay active during COVID-19, but felt less confident exercising independently. The app supports safer walking through route awareness, health-based alerts, and post-activity reflection.

Prototype Research proposal

Context

Older adults needed safer ways to stay active when support systems disappeared.

During COVID-19, many older adults lost access to fitness classes, community routines, and social support systems that made movement feel safer. Independent exercise became more important, but also harder to begin with confidence.

Research signal 43%

of older adults perceived declining physical fitness during the pandemic.

Makizako et al., 2021
How might we

Help older adults stay active safely and independently during periods of isolation?

Research

Research showed that confidence mattered as much as motivation.

We used exploratory research and three user interviews to understand how older adults thought about exercise routines, physical limitations, and barriers to independent activity.

Research included: Exploratory research · 3 user interviews · Affinity mapping · Competitor analysis

Our research pointed to three clear findings:

Exercising felt safer when social support was present

Participants associated exercising with others with greater reassurance, especially when they were uncertain about their physical limits.

This suggested that a digital tool needed to create a sense of guidance, not just track activity.

Fear of injury made independent exercise feel risky

Older adults described hesitation around exercising alone because they were unsure what might happen if they overexerted themselves.

Safety cues and real-time feedback became more important than performance metrics.

Safe exercise needed to reflect personal physical limits

Research showed that different groups require different exercise strategies, and that tailored activity can help prevent injury.

The experience needed to adapt recommendations to each user’s condition and comfort level.

Exploration

We narrowed the opportunity around safer, more informed walking.

Through affinity mapping, we saw a recurring relationship between fear, limited awareness, and the need for more approachable guidance. Walking became the clearest activity to support because it was familiar, accessible, and easier to personalize.

Existing fitness apps did not fully support this audience

We compared MapMyWalk, MyFitnessPal, and Strava against the safety and confidence needs that surfaced in research.

Feature MapMyWalk MyFitnessPal Strava
Designed for older adults No No No
Route preview No No Partial
Real-time safety alerts Premium No Premium
Adaptive recommendations No No No

Early concepts explored before, during, and after activity

Each direction focused on a different moment where confidence could break down.

Iteration

Testing pushed the interface toward clearer guidance and easier choices.

We tested the initial prototype with two older adults using think-aloud tasks, then paired that with a heuristic evaluation. The biggest refinements focused on accessibility, clarity, and reducing uncertainty before action.

01

Improving onboarding clarity

Manual setup originally asked for health information without enough context. The revised flow explains why the app asks for this information and offers optional health sync.

02

Clarifying route information

Route details were hard to scan in the first version. We made route difficulty, distance, and safety context easier to compare before starting.

03

Simplifying the post-workout check-in

The check-in originally felt secondary. The final version gives the reflection step its own screen, with larger choices and clearer progress.

Solution

A walking companion built around awareness, alerts, and reflection.

Trackling helps older adults prepare for walks, monitor their comfort during activity, and reflect afterward so future recommendations can feel safer and more personal.

01 — Onboarding

A personalized start

Users can import health data or complete a simple manual setup, allowing recommendations to begin from their own condition and comfort level.

02 — Alerted routes

Safer route awareness

Trackling recommends nearby routes and surfaces heart-rate alerts so users can understand when to slow down, rest, or choose a gentler path.

03 — Check-in

Reflection that improves future activity

A brief post-activity check-in helps the app learn how the walk felt, creating a feedback loop for more comfortable recommendations over time.

Target heart-rate guidance referenced the American Heart Association’s public target heart-rate ranges.

Reflection

Designing for older adults made accessibility feel immediate.

Trackling was my first experience designing with real user research for an audience I did not personally belong to. It made accessibility feel less like a checklist and more like a core design constraint.

If I revisited the project, I would test earlier with more older adults and spend more time understanding how health data could be translated into plain, useful guidance. The biggest lesson was that confidence is not created by adding more features. It comes from helping people understand what is safe, what is next, and why a recommendation fits them.
